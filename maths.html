Supersingular elliptic curves and quaternion algebras

MMath project, 2026–27

My MMath project is taking me into supersingular elliptic curves and the quaternion algebras that arise from their endomorphisms. This is quite a new direction for me, and I am still very much in the process of learning the arithmetic geometry behind it.

Research in algebra

Quantum knot invariants built from typical representations of a Lie superalgebra

Research internship, summer 2026

Through the AGQ CDT’s Count Me In programme, I studied typical highest-weight representations of Uh(sl(3|1)). I used induced Kac modules and the PBW theorem to construct a basis and determine its weights, before moving on to generator actions and braid-group automorphisms.

This was my first proper encounter with representation theory in a research setting, and a large part of why I became interested in it.

Supervised by Jenny Brown at the University of Edinburgh.

Noetherianity of noncommutative polynomial rings

Third-year project, 2025–26

My third-year project explored skew polynomial rings and formal differential operator rings through the language of Ore extensions. I was particularly interested in what happens to familiar results from commutative algebra when multiplication is no longer commutative.

I looked at Noetherianity, simplicity and the behaviour of prime ideals. This was the project that properly drew me into noncommutative algebra.

Supervised by Dr Omar León Sánchez at the University of Manchester. The project received 86% and was one of the five highest-scoring double projects in my cohort.

Mathematics education

Facilitating effective learning in abstract algebra and proof comprehension

LMS undergraduate research bursary, summer 2025

Supported by an undergraduate research bursary from the London Mathematical Society, my first research project explored how students learn abstract algebra and how teaching can better support the transition to proof-based mathematics.

This was my first real experience of research, and it remains my pride and joy. It led to further work in student partnership, conference presentations and the main case for my Fellowship in Higher Education application.

My work in this area also contributed to the co-authored article “1+1 is greater than 1: Developing Scholarship with Students as Partners” , published in MSOR Connections in 2026.

Supervised by Professor Louise Walker at the University of Manchester.
